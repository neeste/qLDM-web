1. Purpose & What qLDM Measures

qLDM measures monaurally the level of a pure tone (dB SPL RMS) that is judged equal in loudness to a five-tone complex (5TC) centered geometrically on the same frequency (1000 Hz or 4000 Hz):

Δ = (Tone Level at Equal Loudness) − (Complex Level L), in dB SPL RMS.

A positive Δ means across-frequency loudness summation makes the complex louder than an equal-RMS pure tone (requiring a higher-level tone to match it), whereas a negative Δ means the complex is less loud than an equal-RMS tone. In particular, if a listener erroneously matches a single component of the 5-tone complex instead of the whole sound, Δ shifts to ≈ −7 dB (−10 log10 5) across all levels. Everything executes locally in the browser with zero network transmission.

2. Audio Path & 24-Bit Dynamic Range Checklist

A session spans levels from a 30 dB SPL pure tone up to a 100 dB SPL five-tone complex (~10 dB crest factor) plus up to ~14.5 dB of high-frequency insert-earphone roll-off compensation at 5657 Hz (~95 dB peak-to-RMS span). Before calibrating or testing:

  1. Set OS Output Format to 24-bit (or 32-bit float) at 48,000 Hz:
    • macOS: Open Audio MIDI Setup → Select external DAC / audio interface → Format: 48,000 Hz, 2 ch 24-bit Integer (or 32-bit Float).
    • Windows: Sound Control Panel → Playback Device Properties → Advanced → 24 bit, 48000 Hz (Studio Quality).
  2. Disable All OS Sound Processing: Turn off Spatial Audio, loudness equalization, bass boost, and communications ducking.
  3. Set & Confirm Digital Reference and Analog Gain: qLDM defaults to −60.0 dBFS RMS = 60.0 dB SPL (adjustable in calibration from −70 to −50 dBFS). At −60 dBFS, the analog hardware gain is +20.0 dB above qCLS (−40 dBFS), a full-scale (0 dBFS peak) 1 kHz tone is nominally 116.9 dB SPL, and the nominal ER-3A table leaves an unclipped ceiling of 102.3 dB SPL across all stimuli. Because qCLS and qLDM share hardware in a single visit, use a fixed switched attenuator (recommended) or calibrated stepped hardware gain, confirm the qLDM gain at the start of every session, and restore the qCLS gain before running qCLS.
  4. Verify 30 dB SPL Low-Level Resolution: At −60 dBFS, a 30 dB SPL tone has a peak of 1.5 16-bit steps (56–59 dB above the ideal 24-bit floor), so a true 24-bit output path is mandatory. Use the 30 dB Check button in the calibration modal to verify clean resolution of the quietest tone.
  5. Strict Pre-Playback Safety Guard: Before any trial buffer is sent to Web Audio, qLDM inspects the synthesized waveform samples directly and refuses to play any buffer that lacks a component calibration entry, exceeds 0 dBFS (1.0), or exceeds the active ceiling.

3. Per-Component Acoustic Coupler Calibration Protocol

Unlike pure-tone scaling at 1 kHz alone, the 1-octave complex at 4 kHz spans 2828 Hz to 5657 Hz, where insert earphones such as the ER-3A roll off steeply. Measure the table using the exact same coupler and reference convention as your qCLS calibration so levels at 1 and 4 kHz have identical acoustic meaning across both apps:

  1. Connect the test transducer (e.g., Etymotic ER-3A) to a calibrated 2-cc (HA-2) or occluded-ear simulator coupler and sound-level meter.
  2. Click Coupler Calibration & Headroom in the top bar.
  3. Select a starting preset (ER-3A Insert or Flat 0-dB Reference) and set Nominal Tone Level to 80 dB.
  4. Start at 1000.00 Hz, click Play Continuous Tone, adjust your hardware output gain so the sound-level meter reads 80.0 dB SPL, and lock the hardware volume.
  5. Step through each of the 18 pure-tone component frequencies (707.11 Hz to 5656.85 Hz). At each frequency, type the meter reading into Meter Reading (dB SPL) and click Apply to Freq. The tone updates immediately so you can verify the meter now reads within ±0.2 dB of 80.0 dB SPL.
  6. Select the four Coupler Verification Complexes (5TC) at the bottom of the dropdown and confirm overall complex RMS in the coupler is within ±1.0 dB of nominal across levels, checking distortion at 100 dB SPL.
  7. Enter the transducer label and date, then click Save Coupler Calibration. Check the Pre-Session Headroom table on the main screen to confirm all stimuli show OK above Max Output.

4. Listener Instructions & Component-Listening Safeguards

Because the pure tone shares the exact center frequency of the middle component of the 5-tone complex, an analytical listener who tries to “hear out” that middle component instead of judging the whole complex will set the tone ~7 dB too low (10 log10(5) = 7.0 dB) at every level. qLDM enforces four safeguards:

  • Standardized Instruction Gate: Displayed before Practice and repeated before Block 1 (1000 Hz) and Block 2 (4000 Hz):
    “You will hear two sounds. Choose the one that was louder. Judge the loudness of the whole sound, not of a part of it, and ignore differences in pitch or quality.”
  • 8-Trial Practice with Obvious Checks: Trials 1–3 present unmistakable ±10–12 dB level differences and report the listener's score to the tester.
  • Post-Practice Tester Check: Prompts the tester to ask, “What did you compare between the two sounds?” before starting Block 1, with a 1-click option to repeat Practice.
  • Automatic Block Flag & Repeat: If every completed forward 1-octave match in a block has Δ ≤ −6.0 dB, qLDM flags COMPONENT_MATCH_SUSPECTED on those 1-octave matches and provides a 1-click Repeat Block with Instruction button (retaining both passes in the exported CSV/JSON).

5. Listener Limits, Ceiling Bounds, & Output Files

  • Listener Limits: Enter 1 kHz and 4 kHz CU 5 and Too Loud (CU 50) values manually or click Import qCLS_ELC CSV to load them directly from the participant's qCLS equal-loudness contour file. Any fixed complex level below CU 5 + 10 dB or above Too Loud / Max Output is skipped and recorded in LM_PSE with its exact reason.
  • Ceiling Cap & Bound Counts: When the varied sound reaches Max Output (or a ceiling lowered by Too Loud) on three consecutive trials, the match is flagged AT_CEILING (distinct from AT_LIMIT_3_CONSEC for the ±20 dB offset range and 10 dB floor), and LM_PSE records both n_at_ceiling and n_at_ceiling_fixed_louder so downstream NEA inversion can treat capped matches as statistical bounds without excluding or repeating them.
  • In-Trial “Too Loud” Button: Always active during testing; immediately stops playback, lowers the session ceiling by 5 dB for the remainder of the session, and ends any track whose fixed level exceeds the new ceiling.
  • Three Downloads (Client-Side):
    • LM_PSE_[ID]_[L|R]_[YYYYMMDD].csv — One row per match (Δ, tone PSE, curvature SE, shared block σ, n_at_ceiling, n_at_ceiling_fixed_louder, track endpoints, flags, exclusion reasons).
    • LM_TBT_[ID]_[L|R]_[YYYYMMDD].csv — Complete trial-by-trial log (block, pass, track, stimuli, levels, interval order, response, RT in ms, active ceiling).
    • LM_SES_[ID]_[L|R]_[YYYYMMDD].json — Full provenance record (version, RNG seed, analog gain confirmation, digital reference & full-scale 1 kHz SPL, sample rate, audio devices, exact component frequencies & phases, coupler calibration table, headroom limits, and PSE summary).
